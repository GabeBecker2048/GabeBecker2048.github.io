My stance on AI

Last updated November 14, 2026

All text on this page is 100% human written, always.

TL;DR: I use AI assistance in writing code and doing research, in both my professional and personal projects. I never use AI for project assets, such as music or art; I firmly believe that all project assets should be original to the project, another artist's work used with permission, or commissioned from a human artist. In my professional work, I use AI as much as I can without giving up my core understanding of what I'm doing; I never vibe code my way through my job.

My experience with AI

I have a computer science degree from Mississippi State University with a concentration in artificial intelligence, so I studied AI before I ever used it as a tool. In college I built an AI that plays Tetris. Today I use AI daily in my job as a data engineer, where I led my team's research into agentic AI, and in my personal projects.

AI in coding

AI is among the most transformative technologies for programmers since the creation of the internet, for better and for worse. This page explains how I use AI for coding, why I believe it speeds up delivery without sacrificing quality when the person using it understands the code, and why I hold AI in coding and AI in art to different standards.

Vibe coding is defined by some as "using AI to write code". I think this definition is inaccurate; a better one is "shipping AI-generated code without any review".

To see why the two are different, it helps to separate the two parts of creating good software: design and coding. In his 1987 essay "No Silver Bullet: Essence and Accidents of Software Engineering" (Computer, vol. 20, no. 4), Frederick P. Brooks, Jr., a Turing Award winner and the author of The Mythical Man-Month, argued:

I believe the hard part of building software to be the specification, design, and testing of this conceptual construct, not the labor of representing it and testing the fidelity of the representation.

Although the essay was published in 1987, its message still holds: design, not coding, is the most critical and difficult part of building good software. Brooks even considered artificial intelligence and automatic programming as candidate silver bullets, and concluded that neither was one. There is no silver bullet for good software engineering, not even AI.

My own view is the same. Writing the code (the part AI is largely automating away) was never the hardest part of creating good software. It was just the most tedious part. Even in 1987, they knew that!

This is why AI is a multiplier of technical skill. If you are already good at programming and have a strong technical foundation, AI will multiply your productivity, because you know what good software design looks like. However, if you don't understand the technical details of what the AI is writing, you will end up with an unreliable, underspecified, hard-to-extend program.

This is also the difference between vibe coding and how I work. Vibe coding skips the hard part. When I use AI for programming, I design, prototype, research, and plan first. I talk through options and nitpick the AI-generated code until it matches my design and vision. If the AI ever writes a block of code that I don't understand, I push it to explain and justify that code until I do.

None of this means AI comes without a cost. It takes tasks out of my hands, and the things I stop doing are things I slowly stop knowing. For small things, like boilerplate, I accept that. We need to ensure the large things, like how fundamental systems work, don't get forgotten in the same way. It's why I'm glad I learned to code before AI existed: I had to build that understanding the hard way, and I'm not sure that I would have built it at all if I'd had the choice.

AI in art

I am, and always will be, against AI-generated art and music replacing human-made art and music. That being said, I am not strictly "anti-AI" when it comes to art and music; I think AI-generated art and music do have their place. For example, some people (not myself) really enjoy AI art, and we should not police what people are allowed to enjoy. AI can also be a good way to make satire, where the message is more important than the art itself. However, anything AI-generated should be clearly marked, and treated entirely differently from human-created art.

Why AI in art and AI in coding should be held to different standards

Art is a personal expression of emotion, meant to evoke emotion in others. Code is a set of instructions for a computer to carry out. Code is not a work of art that is meant to be experienced.

Additionally, the way a piece of art is created is what makes it special. A stroke of a paintbrush is specific to a person and a work of art; it can never be recreated exactly. The same goes for the tone of an instrument, or the style of an animator. Computer code is not appreciated because of what it is; it is appreciated because of what it does. The creativity in coding lies in deciding what a program should do, and AI doesn't take that away.

How I use AI in my personal projects

Many of my projects predate AI coding tools and have no AI assistance at all. Others I started by hand and then used AI to carry over the finish line, and in some the code was 100% AI-generated.

The following items in my projects are often AI-generated, and are always reviewed by me:

  • READMEs and CHANGELOGs
  • Project wikis and documentation (with many handwritten edits and additions)
  • Code, with the amount of AI-generated vs human-written code varying widely by project

Project assets like art and music are never AI-generated.

How I use AI as a software engineer

As a software engineer, AI is continually transforming my day-to-day, usually for the better. I spearheaded the research into using agentic AI to automate repetitive but complex tasks on my team, and I'm always pushing for more agentic automation at my company.

My team has skills and agents that help with large tasks, such as onboarding new datasets, fixing broken pipelines, and running health checks and maintenance on existing ones.

I am grateful to have AI to accelerate my productivity at work, and I'm always looking for new ways to leverage AI without any loss in quality of deliverables.
